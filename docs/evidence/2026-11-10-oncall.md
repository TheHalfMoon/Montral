# On-call schedules and escalation to people

## Gap
Montral had acknowledgement, reminders and per-project escalation levels that
notified channels only. There were no on-call schedules: nothing could page "whoever
is on call", rotations, overrides (swaps, holidays) and workspace-level escalation
policies did not exist. Ledger item 13; gap matrix row "On-call schedules".

## Design
- **Engine** (`apps/api/src/oncall-engine.ts`): pure functions `whoIsOnCall(schedule, at)`
  and `shiftsBetween(schedule, from, to)`; no database, clock or environment access.
  Handoffs are local wall-clock times in the schedule's IANA zone (Intl only, no
  time-zone library). A handoff time skipped by spring forward happens at the next
  valid instant (the moment the clocks jump, found by bisection between the two
  offsets); a time repeated by fall back happens once, at its first occurrence.
  Rotations: daily, weekly on a weekday (the first shift may be partial when the
  start date is another weekday), or every N hours. Custom rotations shorter than
  24 hours are measured in elapsed time from the first handoff (every slot keeps its
  full length across DST, so a 1-hour rotation never gives anyone a zero-length
  slot on the spring night); daily, weekly and custom rotations of 24 hours or more
  follow the local wall clock. Restriction windows
  (weekdays + HH:MM-HH:MM, crossing midnight allowed) limit a layer. A later layer
  covers earlier ones wherever it has someone; overrides beat every layer; on
  overlap the latest created wins (ties: larger id, so the answer never depends on
  input order). A person no longer in the workspace is skipped: the shift goes to
  the next member in the layer order and their overrides are ignored.
- **Storage** (migration `0053_oncall_escalation`): `oncall_schedule` (org, optional
  project, name, zone, `version`), `oncall_layer` (ordered; CHECKs on rotation type,
  shift length, HH:MM, weekday, date), `oncall_override` (CHECK end > start, user FK
  cascade, created_by), `escalation_policy` (ordered jsonb steps, `repeat_count`
  0-5, `version`), `oncall_calendar_token` (SHA-256 of the token only, revocable),
  `monitor.escalation_policy_id` (FK, set null on delete), and
  `alert_delivery.target_user_id` for people notified directly.
- **Optimistic concurrency**: every schedule/layer/policy write is one conditional
  `UPDATE … WHERE version = $read RETURNING`, layers replaced in the same
  transaction; a stale version gets `409 version_conflict` with `currentVersion`.
- **Escalation**: `claimEscalations` keeps the existing exactly-once claim (a
  conditional UPDATE on the level that was read). A monitor with a policy follows
  it: step `level % n`, round `level / n`; step 1 is due when the incident opens;
  the next step is due `timeoutMin` after the current one; after the last step of
  the last round the clock stops. Monitors without a policy keep the project's
  levels; project levels (and the stored jsonb) accept the old `{channelIds,
  escalateAfterMin}` shape and the new optional `userIds` / `scheduleIds`.
  `sendEscalations` resolves the step's people (named members + whoever the engine
  says is on call, overrides included) and fans out through the existing
  `fanOut`/`queueDeliveries`/`claimDelivery`/`execute` path with stand-in
  "personal" channels (email to the account address; push to that person's
  registered phones only). Retries go through the existing `claimRetries`, which
  rebuilds the stand-in channel from the account and skips people who left.
  Each step writes a team-only (internal) incident update naming who was notified
  and why, plus audit events `incident.escalate` (target: incident) and
  `incident.escalation_notify` (target: each person), actor `system`.
- **Acknowledgement** stops escalation (unchanged) and now assigns the incident to
  the acknowledger only when it has no assignee (`coalesce`). Resolution clears
  every clock (unchanged), and queued escalation retries are skipped (unchanged).
- **Permissions**: owners/admins manage schedules, layers, policies and anyone's
  overrides; members read everything and may add an override for themselves and
  remove one they created for themselves. Project-limited keys read their
  projects' and workspace-wide rows and write only their projects'. Tenant
  isolation: every query is bound to the caller's organization; foreign ids 404.
- **API**: `/api/oncall/{now,members,schedules,…/layers,…/shifts,…/overrides,calendar}`,
  `/api/escalation-policies`, public `GET /api/public/oncall/<token>.ics`
  (rate-limited). Shift ranges are capped at 90 days. MCP: read-only
  `who_is_on_call` and `list_oncall_shifts`. Docs: `docs/api.astro`,
  `docs/mcp.astro`, `docs/self-host.astro#oncall` (English and Chinese).
- **UI**: `/app/oncall` (sidebar, workspace section): on call now, schedule editor
  (layers, rotation order, zone, handoff, restriction windows), next two weeks
  (bar + table), overrides, escalation policy editor, personal calendar link.
  Monitor form: escalation policy selector. Alerting: project levels can page
  on-call schedules. English and Chinese strings.

## Independent review fixes (review of head 0840850)
1. (high) Override volume and evaluation cost: overrides are capped at 500 not yet
   ended per schedule and 50 per person per schedule (counted under a per-schedule
   advisory lock), at most 90 days long, starting at most a year ahead, 60 created
   per person per hour. Override precedence is now one sweep over the sorted
   boundaries with a max-heap (O((L+O) log(L+O)) instead of a scan of every
   override per boundary), zone offsets are cached per 15-minute bucket, and
   paging evaluates only the instant of paging (`onCallAt`, overrides covering that
   instant) instead of a ±14-day window. `whoIsOnCall` widens its window from one
   day only while the shift reaches the edge.
   Measured (local Windows dev machine, Node 24, median of 5/21 runs after warm-up):
   10 layers × 1-hour rotation + 500 overrides over 90 days: `shiftsBetween`
   15 ms; `whoIsOnCall` 0.5 ms; `onCallAt` 0.1 ms. The perf test asserts < 200 ms,
   < 10 ms and < 5 ms, and a brute-force cross-check of override precedence.
2. (medium) Calendar link: created only from a signed-in session (keys/agents 403);
   with a link active, creation needs `replace=true` and the response says
   `replaced: true` / the UI confirms first. The feed lists only the person's own
   shifts; all workspace schedules count because a signed-in member can read every
   project of the workspace (project limits exist only for keys/agents, which
   cannot mint a link). The e2e test checks that none of the other person's shift
   starts appear.
3. (medium) Acknowledgement race: `sendEscalations` re-reads the incident right
   before queueing (an acknowledged/resolved incident gets a skipped row and no
   timeline entry), and the delivery worker re-reads it right before calling the
   provider for escalations and reminders (row ends `skipped`). e2e injects the
   acknowledgement after the claim and after queueing.
4. (medium) `/oncall/members`: sessions get name, email, role; API keys and agents
   only `{ userId, name }`.
5. (low) Short custom rotations at spring forward: rule above; test New York
   2026-03-08 1-hour A/B/C (six full hours, everyone appears).
6. (low) Deleting a policy stops escalation of its open unacknowledged incidents
   (in the same transaction, under the policy row lock) and writes an internal
   timeline update; new incidents use the project's levels.
7. (low) iCal responses are `cache-control: private, no-store`.
8. (low) Acknowledgement assigns only when unassigned (documented in the API and
   self-host docs and the PR); a policy's first step skips channels that already got
   (or are getting) the incident's down alert, with a skipped row.

## Tested
Local (Windows, no PostgreSQL): `pnpm -r typecheck`; `apps/api`
`oncall-engine.test.ts` (38), `oncall.test.ts` (8), and the existing alerts,
scheduler, MCP, audit and guard suites; `apps/web` unit suites.

- Engine unit tests: zone validation; New York spring-forward gap (02:30 → 07:00Z)
  and fall-back overlap (first 01:30); Lord Howe 30-minute gap and overlap; zones
  without DST (Tokyo, UTC, Kolkata) round-trip; real/leap dates; layer validation;
  handoffs exactly at the boundary and 1 ms before; 23.5 h / 25 h shifts across
  New York DST; weekly Sunday 02:30 handoff in the gap; London weekly handoff
  167 h / 169 h across BST start/end; mid-week start; custom 12 h rotation across
  fall back; overrides (span, nested both ways, partial overlap both orders, gap
  filling, creation-time ties); restriction windows (business hours over a 24/7
  base, London summer time, crossing midnight, whole day, invalid layer ignored);
  removed members (rotation skip, empty layer, overrides ignored); leap day; and
  property tests over 40 seeded random schedules (ordering/non-overlap,
  whoIsOnCall = shiftsBetween at random instants and at boundaries, split-range
  invariance, full coverage without restrictions).
- Escalation unit tests (fake repo): on-call + named people resolved, ex-members and
  missing schedules noted; personal deliveries queued with `targetUserId` and no
  channel id; account-language mail (Chinese for a zh-CN user); push only to that
  person's phone; a configured push channel cannot be narrowed to one person by a
  smuggled config; old channel-only steps unchanged; iCal escaping and folding;
  MCP tools are read-only GETs.
- PostgreSQL e2e (`oncall.e2e.test.ts`, CI only, PostgreSQL 17 and 18): CRUD and
  validation; member permissions (403s, own overrides only); tenant isolation
  (outsider gets 404 on every schedule route, empty lists); layer CRUD bumping the
  version; audit events for every change; concurrent PATCHes with the same version
  → exactly one 200 and one 409 with only the winner's change stored, stale layer
  add/delete → 409, three concurrent policy PATCHes → one 200; policy validation
  (empty step, non-member, another workspace's schedule), monitor policy
  selection refused across workspaces, schedule in use → 409, project levels with
  schedules/people and the old shape, policy delete returns monitors to project
  levels; escalation through a schedule with four concurrent `sendEscalations`
  on two connection pools → one delivery to the on-call member; missed
  acknowledgement → next step to the named owner; repeatCount 1 → second round,
  then the clock stops at level 4; timeline entries (internal) and audit events
  per step and per person; an override decides who is paged; acknowledgement
  assigns the incident and stops escalation even with a forced-due clock and two
  racing workers; a second acknowledgement keeps the assignee; a failed personal
  email is retried by `retryDeliveries` and sent on attempt 2; resolution stops
  further steps; calendar feed (token-only access, replacement revokes the old
  link, revoke, unknown and malformed tokens 404, only the person's shifts).
- Cross-browser journey (Chromium, Firefox, WebKit, mobile viewports): the On-call
  page with a schedule and a policy renders, is in the navigation, and passes the
  axe WCAG 2.1 AA scan (`oncall`).

## Not tested / limits
- The rate limit of 60 override creations per hour is not exercised by a test
  (the per-person cap of 50 is reached first in the e2e test).
- PostgreSQL e2e and the browser journey run only in GitHub CI (no Docker or
  PostgreSQL on the development machine).
- A worker that dies between claiming a step and queueing its deliveries loses that
  step (the existing claim model for reminders and escalations); the next step still
  fires after its timeout.
- Step 1 of a policy is paged on the scheduler's next tick (up to one minute after
  the incident opens), not inline.
- Escalation applies to automatic (monitor) incidents, as before; manual incidents
  are not escalated.
- Real APNs and SMTP delivery to people was not exercised (sinks and mocks only).
- Editing a policy (or switching a monitor to another policy) while an incident is
  escalating continues at the current level with the new steps.
- `whoIsOnCall` reports shift start/end clipped to ±14 days around the instant.
- No SMS/voice, no per-person notification rules (delays per channel), no
  follow-the-sun handoff UI beyond layers with restriction windows.
